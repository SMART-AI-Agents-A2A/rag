Abstract

Food security remains a significant global concern, particularly in large countries like Brazil and China. Modern agriculture faces the dual challenge of increasing crop productivity while ensuring environmental sustainability amid climate change.

Precise and smart agriculture management is crucial, leveraging technologies like IoT, 5G/6G, and Artificial Intelligence to enhance decision-making, reduce resource usage, and minimize environmental impact. Innovative agriculture applications generate vast amounts of data from IoT sensors and remote sensing. However, the distributed nature of real-world infrastructures complicates data management. This is where the IoT Computing Continuum, which includes sensors, edge computing nodes, and cloud datacenters, comes into play. It handles the end-to-end data flow, a complex task that requires innovative solutions. Yet, ensuring a reliable dataflow for sophisticated models that enhance precision, food security, and environmental protection remains challenging. The SMART project aims to develop and deploy trustworthy platforms across the IoT computing continuum, from field sensors to the cloud. It will utilize various sensors, data sources, networking technologies, data management, and machine learning to ensure reliable dataflow and trustworthiness.

The project will be piloted in Brazil and China, focusing on coffee and wheat crops.

It will emphasize smart irrigation and fertilization to reduce water and fertilizer usage. This approach aims to contribute to sustainability goals and address the challenges of climate change.

1. Project Basis

1.1. Research Significance

Sustainable agricultural development is crucial to global food security, ecological and environmental protection, and economic growth. However, with the rapid increase in the global population, which is expected to approach 10 billion by 2050, agricultural production is facing unprecedented pressure. It not only needs to meet the growing demand for food but also needs to cope with the challenges brought by climate change, ensure food quality and safety, and maintain ecological balance [1]. There are many problems in the application of agricultural technology, which not only restricts the sustainability of production but also poses a severe threat to the environment and human health.

First, the lack of irrigation efficiency has led to a large amount of water waste, which threatens the sustainability of agricultural production and regional ecological balance. According to the FAO (Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations Global Information System on Water and Agriculture, global agricultural irrigation consumes about 70% of freshwater resources [2]. However, according to the International Water Management Institute (IWMI), the water efficiency of traditional flood irrigation systems is only 35%-40% [3]. This inefficient water resource management, not only due to over-irrigation but also due to backward irrigation technology and improper irrigation management, has caused water evaporation and underground leakage, further exacerbating the global water crisis and posing a considerable challenge to the sustainability of agricultural production and the balance of the ecosystem.

Secondly, the overuse of fertilizers and pesticides has caused severe environmental problems, further threatening the sustainability of agricultural production and ecological health. According to data from the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO), global fertilizer use has increased from about 14 million tons in 1961 to nearly 20 billion tons in 2019. Excessive fertilizer application has led to eutrophication of water bodies, soil acidification, and heavy metal pollution [4]. Similarly, the abuse of pesticides not only causes pesticide residues and increased biological resistance but also harms non-target organisms, resulting in health problems and economic losses of billions of dollars each year due to environmental pollution. In addition, violations in agricultural production, such as the illegal use of banned chemicals and genetically modified seeds, undermine fair market competition and cause serious public health problems and environmental crises.

To address these challenges, the computing continuum, as an emerging technology

system [5], has shown great potential in promoting sustainable agricultural development.

The computing continuum technology brings real-time data collection, analysis, and application to the agricultural field by seamlessly integrating computing resources and services between physical and digital spaces, including edge computing, cloud computing, the Internet of Things (IoT), and Artificial Intelligence (AI) [6], significantly improving the intelligence of agricultural production and resource utilization efficiency.

1) The computing continuum can achieve real-time and geographical distribution of data processing through distributed computing, making the agricultural production process more intelligent and precise. For example, in the intelligent irrigation system, by real-time monitoring of soil moisture and crop water demand, the computing continuum technology can accurately control the

1.3. State of the Art

Smart agriculture represents a new form of growing crops, increasing productivity, and decreasing the use of precious resources (water, land, energy) and chemical substances that harm the environment [11]. The use of technology to add intelligence to agriculture still has a vast potential to generate significant advances. Through continuous technological innovation and application promotion, smart agriculture will gradually change the global agricultural landscape and become an essential pillar of agricultural development in the 21st century. Smart agriculture relies on agricultural sensors to monitor the environment and production factors in real time, such as meteorological conditions (e.g., precipitation, humidity, temperature), water resources (e.g., water level, flow), field conditions (e.g., soil moisture, temperature) and the operation of the irrigation system (e.g., pressure, flow) [12]. These data are stably and reliably transmitted through wireless or wired communication networks and then intelligently analyzed and processed by the agricultural Internet of Things platform [5]. Precision and efficient irrigation is an important part of water and fertilizer management in the production process of smart agriculture [13]. It is an important link in developing high-water-efficiency agriculture and improving agricultural water use efficiency on a large scale. Through monitoring, transmission, diagnosis, decision-making, and dynamic management of crop water, comprehensive use of natural precipitation and precision irrigation strategies, adaptive irrigation control based on the water demand of crops throughout the growth period is achieved to ensure that crops grow under the most suitable water conditions, achieving comprehensive benefits such as water saving, fertilizer saving, labor saving, and environmental protection [14][15].

2. Research Objectives, Content, and Approach

2.1. Research Objectives and Key Issues

The SMART project aims to build an efficient, accurate, and sustainable agricultural supervision and management technology system based on the computing continuum in response to the urgent need for the integrated development of global smart agricultural supervision, combining the technological advantages of Brazil and China. A globally applicable agricultural data collection and processing solution is formed by developing a computing continuum perception integration technology with high compatibility, high reliability, and autonomous decision-making capabilities, integrating multisource information collection equipment from the air, sky, and ground with edge computing technology. At the same time, high-precision crop status intelligent perception technology and intelligent decision-making algorithm models are developed to achieve all-around monitoring and prediction of crop growth status and support precision agricultural practices such as water-saving irrigation, environmentally friendly fertilization, and green spraying. Specific goals include:

1) Build a computing continuum perception integration technology with high

compatibility and reliability, achieve extensive support for protocol standards of different countries and regions, and ensure seamless integration of selforganizing networks and edge computing of high-altitude, low-altitude, and surface information collection equipment.

2) Develop terminal (end-device) computing technology with autonomous

decision-making capabilities, reduce the burden of data transmission by realizing intelligent computing and decision-making in drones and other aggregation nodes, and improve the equipment's autonomous cruising and automatic fault identification and repair capabilities.

2.2.2. Terminal Computing

For the SMART project, "terminal computing" means the set of IoT devices (things) that operate near the end-user and collectively perform data collection (or perception) activities. This technology mainly focuses on realizing intelligent computing and decision-making (i.e., edge computing technology) on terminal computer nodes (such as drones, aggregation nodes, etc.), aiming to reduce the amount of data transmission, improve system response speed, and realize automatic detection and repair of anomalies, thereby enhancing the overall intelligence and reliability of the system. neural networks (GNN) [51], which in recent years have become a cutting-edge

technology for processing graph data due to its application in deep learning.

• Air-ground-space collaborative information collection involves deploying a

cloud-edge framework and a bandwidth allocation algorithm. Traditionally, edge

devices upload a large amount of data to the cloud, which leads to huge

communication overhead and increases network delay. SMART will implement

an integrated continuum collaborative framework by integrating multilevel data

collection systems in the air and on the ground. Satellites perform large-scale

monitoring and provide environmental data from a macro perspective, drones

perform a more detailed local data collection, and ground equipment such as

unmanned vehicles and sensor networks continuously monitor soil parameters and

microclimate conditions. Filtering techniques based on an IoT trustworthiness

framework will be used for that purpose [52]. Also, the bandwidth allocation

between each upper-layer device and the lower-layer device is limited, which

limits the data transmission rate. To address this problem, the project will develop a bandwidth allocation algorithm based on reinforcement learning.

• Management and monitoring system based on the computing continuum: the

SMART project will adopt the IoT Computing Continuum (IoTinuum) with six

stages [5] together with the IoT Architecture for Smart Applications (IoTecture) [40] so that we can precisely determine which architectural components (software, hardware, communication) will be deployed and their arrangement (Thing, Mist, Fog, Cloudlet, Cloud, App).

2.3.2. Accurate and Stable Perception of Agriculture Production

Due to the complexity and diversity of the terrain, landforms, and climate of the agricultural production environment in traditional agricultural production for a specific crop, farmers often rely on past planting experience to manage the crop. The SMART project will develop an accurate and stable farmland production perception platform.

• Crop growth environmental data based on multisource sensors: the project will

collect environmental data such as air temperature, air humidity, wind speed, wind direction, solar radiation, UV intensity, CO2 concentration, oxygen concentration, pH value, soil temperature, soil moisture, and soil nutrients through different sensors including UV, humidity, temperature, light intensity, and soil moisture.

Also, crop growth status data is based on image information: a combination of

UAVs, ground cameras, and multispectral cameras to collect plant growth status

information, such as leaf area index, crop height, and canopy coverage.

• Pest monitoring data based on air-ground multisource information: In the aerial detection of pest detection, this module uses a UAV thermal infrared camera and a high-resolution RGB camera to capture pest information over a wide area. The RGB camera captures visible light images of the crop surface to identify noticeable pest features, leaf spot areas, and pest symptoms. Thermal infrared cameras detect temperature anomalies on the crop surface, where elevated temperatures indicate plant damage due to insect infestation.

2.3.3. Models for Sustainable Agriculture Management

Intelligent regulation of sustainable farmland water, fertilizer, and pesticide is integral to optimizing water-saving irrigation, environmentally friendly fertilizer, and green pesticide application. This process requires a combination of ground sensors, remote sensing data, and crop growth models to systematically manage the growing environment of crops through algorithms and rules to ensure rational use of resources and maximize crop yield and quality. SMART will build a green management model to realize the intelligent regulation of water, pesticide, and fertilizer integration.

• Decision rule system for water, pesticide, and fertilizer. Constructing a crop growth model to simulate the growth process and predict the growth results under different management strategies can help farmers and agricultural managers make scientific decisions according to various environmental conditions and management strategies to improve crop yield and resource utilization efficiency.

• Algorithms and Models for Sustainable Farmland Management: multiple data

above. These models deal with irrigation, fertilization, and pesticide application.

A sustainable and smart farmland management system based on the computing

• System design and technical architecture: The design concept of the system is

based on the computing continuum - a multistage computing architecture that

integrates cloud computing, fog computing, and edge computing, aiming at

decentralization of data processing and real-time response to tasks. The system

architecture consists of three main components: a data acquisition stage, a data processing stage, and an execution control stage.
